## 1. Researched evidence

- **The loss.** KUSCCO lost KSh13.3bn to fraud; PwC's forensic audit found it insolvent by KSh12.5bn,
  with KSh24.8bn of SACCO deposits at risk; a deceased auditor's signature appeared on the books; 247 of
  257 licensed deposit-taking SACCOs lost money; a dissolution meeting was set for 28 August 2026
  (10-R9, 13-SSA; money254.co.ke, nation.africa, allafrica.com, theweeklyvisionews.net; all search excerpts in 10-R9's sources).
- **Sector damage.** 201 SACCOs lost about 10% of core capital and SASRA allowed staggered loss
  recognition; Tower Sacco alone lost KSh149.5m (03-G4, 10-R9; businessdailyafrica.com, capitalfm.co.ke excerpt).
- **Reforms in train.** A deposit protection and savings stabilisation fund, mandatory digital reporting,
  shared digital platforms, a Central Equity Facility, fit-and-proper tests (10-R9; kenyanews.go.ke, sasra.go.ke).
- **Workflow.** Members saw quarterly statements, SASRA saw returns from the apex; the fraud sat above the
  core-banking layer and external audit rested on one signature (10-R9, 03-G4).
- **Proof of reserves needs an off-chain attestor** because reserves are bank balances (03-F3, 10-R9), which is why the custodian bank signs.
- **Platform facts.** Soroban custom accounts enforce auth in `__check_auth` with Ed25519 signers; fee-bump
  sponsorship through the OpenZeppelin Relayer Channels plugin; resource ceilings, state archival, no native cron (01-§6, 01-§14).
- **Prior art on Stellar** is issuer- or member-level only (ReserveProof, CoopFin, ZK proof-of-reserve hackathon repos); none targets the apex layer (10-R9, 11-R11).
- **Portfolio history.** Ranked #8 of 8 in 10-R9's top eight "because reserves are off-chain, a permissioned SASRA database could do most of it, and the buyer is a regulator".

### The redesign table and its source status

The register tests the apex's **assets** (borrowers confirm loans, the bank confirms cash), because the
reported failure was on the asset side. The mapping is a team **assumption**; each row rests on search
excerpts and must be checked against the PwC report (verify-first item (d)).

| Reported audit finding | What the register would show | Caught? | Source status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loans to dormant or fictitious SACCOs | Counterparty unregistered or inactive: nobody can confirm; the line stays out of coverage and is listed unconfirmed | Yes | Search excerpt |
| Concealed inter-departmental lending | Cash has left, so the custodian balance is lower; if booked as loans to departments, those lines are unconfirmed | As a coverage gap, not a named loan | Search excerpt |
| Commission 1% → 3%, cash lost | Cash leaves the custodian account; coverage drops that period | As a coverage gap | Search excerpt |
| Income overstated | Coverage uses no income figure | Not flagged directly; cannot hide a gap | Search excerpt |
| Forged auditor signature | No auditor signature is needed for the ratio | Vector removed | Researched (10-R9) |
| Real loans in arrears | Borrowers confirm balance and arrears; loans over 90 days are excluded | Yes | Search excerpt |
| Insider loan to a colluding member SACCO | The colluding board confirms it | **No** | Assumption |

## 2. Observed facts (this build, this environment)

- `cargo test`: 46 `apex_register` tests (unit, negative, auth, vectors, a real two-signature
  `board_account` auth entry, a 200-case property test, the seeded `scenario_kuscco_pattern`) and 8
  `board_account` tests pass in the Soroban host. `test_snapshots/` are generated by these runs.
- `stellar contract build` produces `apex_register.wasm` (≈ 42 KB) and `board_account.wasm` (≈ 6 KB).
- `cd app && npm test`: 111 offline `node --test` tests pass, including vectors shared with Rust and
  `coverage.json` exported by the Rust property test.
- `apex simulate` reproduces `data/seed/expected.json` in every report field.
- **Not observed:** any network behaviour. Stellar testnet, Friendbot and Horizon were unreachable, so
  `scripts/deploy-testnet.sh` and `scripts/e2e-testnet.sh` were written but never run, and the live
  client's RPC path is untested. Transaction entry limits (verify-first (c)) were not measured.

## 3. Team assumptions

- A permissioned SASRA database would deliver most of the value (the panel's refutation, accepted).
- Boards authorise with 2 of 3 officers (chair, treasurer, secretary); SACCO by-laws were not researched.
- A monthly period with a 10-day response window and a 5-day custodian window.
- Loans more than 90 days in arrears are non-performing and excluded from coverage.
- The custodian's role is modelled on bank confirmation letters under ISA 505; no bank has been asked.
- Concentration thresholds (`data/seed/concentration.json`: loans > 3× deposits, one loan > 5% of the book,
  a recent loan > 1% of the book) are uncalibrated.
- The confirmation mechanism is an auditor's external confirmation run every period by the counterparties.

## 4. Hypotheses

- SACCO boards will sign periodic confirmations; finance managers will run `apex check`.
- Members and SASRA value independence from each other enough to prefer a shared ledger.
- A coverage gap would have been visible within one period of a hole opening (baseline at KUSCCO: years).
- Members will accept their responded amounts being public under a member number.
- A core-banking vendor would ship member-side confirmation as a feature.

## 5. Simulated / demo data

Everything in `data/seed/` is generated by `app/src/seed/generate.ts` from a fixed seed and labelled
"(simulated)": a 159-line apex book for 2026-08-31 (39 deposits, 120 loans; thousands separators, `KES`
prefixes, a one-decimal amount, a blank line, trailing spaces, name typos), a dirty copy with a duplicate
reference, a 214-movement custodian statement (reversals, value-date differences, a decimal comma), 40
member ledgers, a response plan and a smaller September period. Planted cases: five fictitious loans to
non-members 901–903 and dormant member 38, two overstated loans (members 5 and 19), an understated deposit
(14), an omitted deposit (33), two late confirmers (11, 29), a non-responder (22) and a colluding insider
loan (37). Result: **confirmed coverage 7807 bps vs booked 10251 bps**. These numbers illustrate the
mechanism only.

## 6. Actual validation

**None yet.** No conversation, pilot, desk replay or payment has happened.

## Baseline and success metric

- **Primary outcome (not measurable in the MVP):** time from a hole opening in an apex's books to a flag
  visible to members and the regulator. Baseline at KUSCCO: years (search excerpt: at least six years of
  overstated profits). Target: one confirmation period (Hypothesis).
- **MVP-level (Simulated, asserted by tests):** 100% of planted fictitious loans excluded from coverage;
  both overstated balances cut to the acknowledged amount; the booked-vs-confirmed gap reported exactly
  (10251 vs 7807 bps); every active member ends each period responded, omitted-claimed or flagged overdue;
  the colluding loan passes and the concentration heuristic flags member 37 (not a performance claim).
- **After validation:** share of a real apex's loan book, by value, that borrowers confirm in the first
  period, and share of members responding on time.

## Experiment plan

**Week 1: sources and 8–10 conversations.**
- Obtain the PwC forensic audit summary or full reporting of it; check the redesign table row by row.
- Reach finance managers at 4–5 DT-SACCOs that disclosed KUSCCO losses, two board chairs or treasurers,
  one external auditor of SACCOs, one core-banking vendor (Kwara or Mombo), and SASRA supervision or ICT staff.
- Ask what the SACCO received from the apex and how it checked it; whether its auditor sent confirmation
  requests; what would have told it earlier; and the four project-specific questions:
  1. Would your board sign a confirm-or-dispute of your apex lines every month, and at what time cost?
  2. Would you accept your confirmed apex balance being readable on a public ledger under a member number?
  3. (SASRA) Would the regulator accept or mandate a register it does not run, or build the same thing in
     its shared platform? If it will build it, this becomes an open specification and reference
     implementation, and the chain becomes optional.
  4. Is there an apex or central finance facility that will take member deposits again, and who runs it?
- Output: named, consented notes (evidence level Observed).

**Week 2: desk replay.** With one SACCO's permission, run `apex check` on its historical apex statements
and its own ledger and record what it would have confirmed or disputed. Separately reconstruct, from the
audit's published figures, what coverage would have shown in the fraud years (a reconstruction, not a
finding). Ask one auditor to review `docs/COVERAGE.md` and `docs/THREAT-MODEL.md`.

**Willingness to pay.** Ask SASRA or the platform operator, the vendor, and SACCOs through their association
who would pay (licence, per-member fee, grant-funded public good). No figure is assumed.

## Killer questions

1. **Would the user care if it disappeared?** Plausible from the evidence (247 of 257 SACCOs exposed, no
   independent check existed), untested. Nobody has used it.
2. **Did anyone outside the team use it?** No.
3. **Before/after measured?** No. The baseline (years to detection) is researched; nothing is measured.
4. **Value lost without AI?** None: there is no AI by design. Confirmations are board decisions, the ratio
   is arithmetic, and the concentration flag is a deterministic rule.
5. **Reason to keep using after the demo?** Hypothesis: a monthly regulatory or apex requirement to confirm.
   Without a mandate or an apex that needs members' trust back, there is none. Killer question 1 above tests it.
6. **Would someone pay?** Hypothesis. Candidates are SASRA's platform, an apex or central facility, a
   core-banking vendor, or a grant for an open standard. No buyer has been asked.
7. **Does the chain create value?** A little. Every property also works in a SASRA-run database; the ledger
   only removes the need to trust the database operator, so members can check the ratio independently of
   both the apex and SASRA. Whether anyone values that is a validation question (`docs/NO-CHAIN-ALTERNATIVE.md`).
8. **Why this chain?** Soroban custom accounts give a 2-of-3 board as a native signing identity enforced by
   the host, fee sponsorship means boards need no XLM, fees are trivial, and the funding path (SCF,
   Instawards) is on Stellar. Arbitrum would work technically (EIP-712 from Safe multisigs); the reasons are
   portfolio and ecosystem ones, not capability. KES anchors are not a reason: no money moves.
